## 2. Economics without notation
When firms compete across regional boundaries, stronger product-market substitutability can change whether a local government offsets a rival productivity-raising green policy by cutting or expanding its own productive infrastructure. The direction reflects both firms' adjustments of ordinary and green investment and a government trade-off between market surplus and delegated territorial environmental targets. In an illustrative region, the response reverses as competition rises. The model does **not** say governments should adopt that reaction or that following it benefits residents or the climate.

## 7. Editor-facing value case (strongest positive argument, ~150 words)
Local governments pursue industrial decarbonization with combinations of firm support and shared infrastructure, while the supported firms compete outside local administrative boundaries. Fiscal competition research already studies taxes and public inputs; environmental oligopoly theory already studies green and cost-reducing private investment. The paper's distinct contribution is a tractable sufficient-condition theorem showing that product substitutability can make the *same local infrastructure policy reaction* switch from a negative to a positive response to neighboring productivity-raising green support. The switch is not inferred from an interior stationary policy alone: for a rational parameterization, the paper verifies a true global subgame-perfect equilibrium against deviations inducing different downstream market structures. Removing conventional investment at matched primitives eliminates the switch, but not universally. The mechanism persists locally under price competition and partial profit leakage. The result yields a precise testable warning: empirical regional-policy-reaction studies should not assume a single constant coefficient between neighboring subsidy and infrastructure choices.

**Assessment of this defense:** real, falsifiable positive-theory content; arguably publishable as a tight note. It is nevertheless weaker than a compelling full-length fiscal-federalism economic or welfare result.

## 8. Skeptical editor refusal (strongest negative argument, ~135 words)
This article combines well-understood features of fiscal competition, public inputs, differentiated Cournot markets, and private R&D in a quadratic game. A sign change in a two-instrument response vector is a routine possibility under inverse-Hessian comparative statics, and the canonical single crossing relies on selected environmental-target intensity and sufficient Bernstein inequalities. The model offers no welfare ranking or coordinated corrective institution linked to the sign-switch threshold; indeed its welfare exercise must be restricted to symmetric policies because an admissible asymmetric monopoly policy yields much higher aggregate welfare and sharply conflicting regional gains. The rival subsidy is not strategically special relative to rival infrastructure in the reduced model, and the slope holds a rival endogenous action fixed rather than characterizing a real exogenous reform's Nash-policy effects. These limitations leave little beyond a rigorous, parameterized illustration for a full public-finance article.

**Decision on objection:** not rebutted by existing certified results. More robustness computations, a different journal label or stronger prose do not remove it.

## 9. Conditional policy WHY–SHOULD–COULD

A clearly positive diagnostic paper is allowed without these, but its standalone field contribution must then be strong enough. Under this skeptical review it is not yet strong enough for ITPF full length.
