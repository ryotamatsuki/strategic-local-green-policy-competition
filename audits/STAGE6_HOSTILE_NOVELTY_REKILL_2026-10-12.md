## 1. Executive verdict
The original broad contributions (multi-instrument fiscal competition; public infrastructure under product-market competition; green subsidies as strategic trade policy; simultaneous green/conventional R&D; tax/subsidy–infrastructure interaction; strategic-complement/substitute reversals; generic LQ Hessian/IFT signs; threshold effects *per se*) are **not new**. They must not be sold as contributions.

A narrower economics theorem is not yet shown to be a direct specialization of a located predecessor: in a three-stage two-jurisdiction policy/investment/differentiated-Cournot game, the *same own infrastructure best response to a rival productive-green-policy shock* changes from negative to positive **exactly once** as product substitutability rises, under primitive sufficient conditions, with the canonical witness certified against global kink/monopoly deviations on an open primitive set. The matched no-x comparison separates outcomes at **the same remaining primitives** but does not prove conventional R&D universally necessary.

The exact quartic and Bernstein uniqueness argument are computational/proof methods, not a general methodological innovation. The LQ inverse-Hessian comparative-static formula is standard; therefore the substantive claim is only the **primitive-to-sign topology and global-equilibrium bridge**, not the abstract fact that a strategic response can switch sign. The case for a full-length field-journal article remains materially weaker than the case for mathematical correctness. Stage 7.5 must independently decide whether it warrants full-paper investment.

This is a **bounded search verdict**, not an exhaustive proof that no similar result exists in all published or working-paper literature. In particular Conrad–Seitz (1997) full text was not accessible in this pass; journal metadata/abstract alone cannot provide a complete proposition-level absorption certificate.

## 4. Canonical-form / general parent-theorem test
Set local vector `z_i=(s_i,h_i)'`, and rival productive-policy index `y_j=(mu/kg)s_j+nu*h_j`. On an interior regular branch, after investing firms are solved out, the own-government objective is LQ:
`W_i(z_i,y_j)=c_i(y_j,theta)+ell_i(theta)'z_i - 1/2*z_i'A_i(theta)z_i + z_i'b_i(theta)y_j`, `A_i=-H_{z_i z_i}` positive definite.

Thus
`z_i^BR=A_i(theta)^(-1)[ell_i(theta)+b_i(theta)y_j]`
and
`d h_i^BR/d s_j=(mu/kg)*e_2' A_i(theta)^(-1)b_i(theta)`.

For `A_i=[[a11,a12],[a12,a22]]` the sign is exactly the sign of `a11*b2-a12*b1` (assuming mu>0, det(A)>0).

**Absorbed by parent theory:** matrix inverse identity, strategic complement/substitute language, possibility of sign reversals when underlying coefficients change, and elementary monotone-polynomial unique-zero tests.

**Not automatically absorbed:** the *economic primitive mapping* from government/firm/Cournot/emissions parameters to the sign expression; the all-theta single-crossing conditions for that specific map; proof that the relevant branch survives globally against nonnegative monopoly/kink deviations. These require candidate-specific calculations and are not an immediate substitution into a located published parent theorem.

**Critical limitation:** `s_B` and `h_B` enter A's continuation only via `y_B`, so `d z_A^BR/d h_B=(nu*kg/mu) d z_A^BR/d s_B`. Consequently **NO rival-subsidy-specific causal channel is identified**. The theorem should be positioned as a response to a rival *productive green-policy index*, with subsidy merely the normalization chosen for differentiation. Own `s_A` vs `h_A` remain economically non-equivalent due to investment costs, emissions, direct infrastructure cost, and government welfare.

### B. Economic consequence
The putative finding is *not* a normative optimal-subsidy prescription. It is a positive result that changing product substitutability can change the direction of decentralized intergovernmental public-input policy reactions even with fixed plant locations and no fiscal-budget coupling, because own policy margins interact with a composite of producer-surplus, consumer-surplus, territorial-target and firm investment feedback. This allows a non-monotone/threshold hypothesis, but empirical identification would require observed differentiated-product substitution, meaningful changes in rival green support, and discrimination against other causes of heterogeneous fiscal reactions. The exact value theta*=.7738 is a canonical normalization, NOT an estimated policy cutoff.

### C. Editor-facing pitch (approx. 150 words)
Decentralized climate-related industrial policy is not a one-instrument subsidy contest: governments can subsidize firms' green investment while also supplying productive infrastructure, and their firms compete beyond local boundaries. Existing fiscal-competition studies characterize multi-instrument reactions, while environmental IO studies examine firms' conventional and green investment. What remains unclear is whether stronger competition systematically changes the *direction* in which a government adjusts infrastructure to rival productive support. We derive an explicit primitive sufficient-condition theorem that predicts a single reversal as product substitutability increases. Unlike comparative statics calculated only on an interior stationary branch, the canonical example survives all nonnegative unilateral policy deviations, including those inducing a change from duopoly to limit-pricing or monopoly. A matched same-primitives counterfactual attributes the sign difference to adding a conventional R&D adjustment margin in that region, without asserting universal necessity. The payoff for fiscal-federalism readers is a falsifiable nonlinear response prediction and an explanation of why simple policy-matching regressions may conceal offsetting investment and environmental-target channels.

### D. Strongest skeptical-editor rejection (approx. 125 words)
The paper appears to place established government tax/public-input response functions inside an established differentiated-Cournot investment framework. Linear-quadratic reaction slopes and zero crossings follow routinely from inverse-Hessian algebra; a unique quartic root may be viewed as a parameterized example rather than a general economic principle. Nearby studies already obtain changes in public investment from fiscal competition, heterogeneous strategic substitutes/complements, and R&D responses indexed by product substitutability. In this paper, the rival green subsidy and rival infrastructure are observationally equivalent cost-shifting indices, limiting the claimed instrument specificity. The policy game does not predict a change in *equilibrium* policy allocations following a feasible exogenous reform, and the welfare benchmark can raise emissions. Unless the primitive sufficient conditions correspond to an economically substantial, interpretable region, the correct and carefully verified theorem could be too narrow for a full-length fiscal-federalism article.

### E. Policy-facing WHY / SHOULD / COULD
- WHY: strategic reaction and cross-jurisdiction welfare wedge derived. **MODELED**.
- SHOULD: restricted welfare coordination gives example but cannot be presented as emissions optimal or universally welfare ranking. **DERIVED, PARTIAL**.
- COULD: legal delegation, fiscal authority, budget/financing incidence, instrument implementation and equilibrium after policy intervention unmodeled. **OUT OF SCOPE for diagnostic theory**. Need affirmative case for why a positive-theory article stands independently.
- Do not translate a partial BR slope into advice that any real government should increase/decrease its infrastructure spending.

4. **Instrument identity**: the rival shock enters via scalar `y_B`; claim instrument specificity only on the *responding* jurisdiction's policy allocation, never the *shocking* jurisdiction's instrument.
5. **Editorial completeness**: the introduction's normative "Should" must not masquerade as welfare-optimal policy, and best-response slope should not be called an equilibrium response to an actual exogenous reform.
