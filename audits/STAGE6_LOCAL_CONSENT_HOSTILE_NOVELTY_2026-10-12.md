## 3. Application-neutral parent class: why the symmetry test is generic
On the active-duopoly branch, after substituting producer investments and Cournot prices, `F(z_A,z_B;theta)=W_A+W_B` is exchange-invariant and QUADRATIC in the two local instrument vectors `z_i=(s_i,h_i)∈R_+^2`. Set
`z_A=v+delta,` `z_B=v-delta`.
Exchange invariance requires `F(v,delta;theta)=f(v;theta)+(1/2) delta' H_-(theta) delta`, with no linear or odd-in-delta terms and no mixed v-delta quadratic term (all vanish under delta→-delta). Hence:
- symmetric active-coordination policy is *stationary* in delta for **any** such symmetric two-region quadratic game;
- if `H_-` has a positive eigenvalue and the nonnegativity, active-firm, and both IR inequalities are slack at the symmetric candidate, an arbitrarily small corresponding antisymmetric delta STRICTLY improves welfare; no specialized theorem is required for this logical step;
- if `H_-` negative definite and the full F Hessian negative definite, the symmetric candidate is uniquely optimal ON THAT branch (subject to feasibility), but this says nothing about other monopoly/kink branches or IR participation there;
- sign changes of `det(H_-)` as a parameter varies are generic parametric LQ spectral crossings. The specific sixth-degree polynomial is an evaluated model-specific formula, NOT a general symmetry-breaking theorem.

Thus T2's **existing** evidence is a direct instantiation of generic symmetric-LQ curvature. To become substantively new, it must produce a nontrivial primitive policy result about the GLOBAL IR-optimal regime and its welfare/distribution consequences that parent examples or nested models cannot already produce. The fact that the papers do not print this exact polynomial is not novelty.

## 5. Economic contribution completeness / editorial challenge

Two policy instruments and dual private investment give a tractable nonnegative-market-game setting where voluntary coordination retains both firms, and increased product substitutability changes whether identical policies are locally welfare-maximizing. An exact global example protects the conclusion against monopoly/kink deviations. If a primitive-level IR-constrained symmetry-selection result can be established beyond the given finite points, the article could explain why more competition can promote policy harmonization in some markets but not others, even when all regions start ex ante alike.

**Skeptical editor rejection (currently stronger)**: Environmental plant allocation with side-payment requirements is at least three decades old; infrastructure centralization and its uneven welfare effects are already standard. Symmetric governments choosing asymmetric policies and product-substitution-dependent investment-subsidy welfare are also published. The surviving novel-seeming sign pattern follows immediately from the antisymmetric block of an exchange-invariant quadratic objective, and its two roots do not certify the globally chosen policy under participation constraints. The key production-efficiency-versus-participation result remains after green investment's competitiveness benefit is deleted, undermining a distinct environmental-industrial-policy narrative. The commitment device for voluntary coordination is assumed but not modeled. Without a general global cooperative policy theorem, the current construction adds exact arithmetic and numerical parameters rather than an economically important new general principle.
