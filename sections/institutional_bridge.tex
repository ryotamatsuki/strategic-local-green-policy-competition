\section{Institutional and Empirical Bridge}
\label{sec:institutional}

The model abstracts from any single national program, but its two policy instruments correspond to an increasingly common distinction in place-based green industrial policy. Governments can support a particular firm's decarbonizing capital expenditure, and they can invest in shared local inputs such as clean-power access, grid capacity, hydrogen or utility networks, ports, industrial land, and related infrastructure. The first instrument primarily changes the private return to green investment; the second can simultaneously improve local production conditions and environmental performance.

Japan's recent green industrial-location policy illustrates this institutional combination. The Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry's GX Strategic Area framework seeks new industrial clusters around assets such as existing industrial complexes and geographically concentrated decarbonized power, including integrated development of power and communications infrastructure \citep{METIGX2026}. A related GX regional investment-support scheme provides a firm-facing investment channel within the same broader place-based transition architecture \citep{METIGXSubsidy2026}. More generally, the OECD reports that subnational governments have an important implementation role in Japan's climate strategy and that roughly 60 percent of local governments have committed to net zero by 2050 \citep{OECDJapan2025}. These examples motivate the coexistence of firm-specific support and shared green infrastructure; they are not used to calibrate the model.

The parameter $\mu>0$ should likewise be read as a scope condition, not as a claim that every environmental expenditure is privately profitable. The mechanism is relevant when at least some green investment also affects product-market competitiveness---for example through energy efficiency, process modernization, lower exposure to energy costs, or product attributes valued by customers. If green investment is pure compliance expenditure with no competitiveness effect, Corollary~\ref{cor:mu-zero} shows that the cross-jurisdictional product-market channel vanishes.

\subsection{Empirical predictions}

The theory generates several predictions that can be taken to data without requiring direct observation of every structural parameter.

First, a rival jurisdiction's firm-specific green support should be associated with a more positive local infrastructure response in industries with stronger product-market substitutability. In a jurisdiction--industry--year panel, the natural reduced-form object is an interaction between a spatially weighted rival green-subsidy measure and an industry-level competition proxy.

Second, the switching threshold should be lower where shared green infrastructure has a larger productivity effect. Exposure to electricity-intensive production, hydrogen or steam networks, port access, grid constraints, or other common industrial inputs can provide empirical variation in the analogue of $\nu$.

Third, switching should be more likely when firms can flexibly adjust conventional productivity investment. The model predicts a lower threshold when $k_x$ is smaller. Measures of capital adjustment, automation intensity, or the responsiveness of ordinary investment to demand shocks could therefore be used to split industries by the strength of this margin.

Fourth, the response should be weak or absent where environmental investment has little effect on marginal cost or product-market position, the empirical counterpart of $\mu\simeq0$.

These predictions suggest a spatial policy-reaction design rather than a simple regression of emissions on subsidies. A credible empirical implementation would distinguish predetermined economic exposure to rival jurisdictions from endogenous policy matching, and would exploit policy discontinuities, eligibility rules, or staggered program changes where available. The present paper remains theoretical, but the predictions clarify which observed policy reactions would be diagnostic of the proposed mechanism rather than generic interjurisdictional competition.
