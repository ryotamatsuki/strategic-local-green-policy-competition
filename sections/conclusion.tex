\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A local government's infrastructure response to a rival's green-investment subsidy can change sign as products become closer substitutes, even when the rival's infrastructure is held fixed. The relevant effect is the derivative after the local government reoptimizes \emph{both} its subsidy and infrastructure. In the canonical comparison the direct cross effect remains positive at low and high rivalry, yet the profiled response changes from negative to positive: producer-surplus, consumer-surplus and territorial-target forces interact with the adjustment of the local subsidy. Sufficient polynomial restrictions establish a unique crossing on the interior branch, and a separate nonnegative-game analysis establishes a genuine global policy SPNE at the rational canonical configuration and on a nonempty open primitive neighborhood.

That distinction is bounded. The target-weight boundary outside the certified neighborhood is an interior response diagnostic, not a global policy-equilibrium classification; some apparently valid signs correspond to exclusionary deviations or infeasible infrastructure. A matched benchmark without conventional investment lacks switching, but conventional investment is not necessary in general. A feasible coordinated active-duopoly welfare comparison can yield higher emissions. The model consequently supplies a falsifiable, conditional cross-policy reaction to a normalized rival competitiveness shock, not a universal infrastructure prescription or direct empirical claim. Existing fiscal-competition research already establishes cross-instrument strategic reactions and several kinds of sign reversal, so the economic importance of the remaining target-sensitive result must be assessed on that narrower basis.
