\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A jurisdiction's response to a rival green-investment subsidy need not have one direction across product markets. With conventional and green firm investment endogenous, competing product-market and territorial-target forces can make the local infrastructure best-response derivative change from negative to positive exactly once as products become closer substitutes. Under the stated sufficient conditions the interior derivative has this sign pattern; a separate full nonnegative-game certification connects it to a global policy SPNE at a rational canonical configuration and on a nonempty open neighborhood. The result is a specific conditional prediction, not an unrestricted theory of fiscal competition or equilibrium across all parameter values.

Its interpretation is equally bounded. Environmental-target intensity can eliminate the reversal, conventional investment is not universally necessary, and the modeled welfare gains from policy coordination need not mean lower emissions. Fixed plant locations, simplified fiscal authority, and the absence of causal evidence also limit immediate policy application. The lesson is therefore not that governments should mechanically match or expand green infrastructure, but that a rival subsidy can change the direction of the optimal cross-instrument response through joint private-investment and public-objective feedback. This is the narrower question that future comparative and empirical work can test.
