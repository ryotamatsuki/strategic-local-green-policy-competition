\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Even with identical product-market substitutability, a government's productive infrastructure response to a marginal rival green-investment subsidy can have opposite signs in two globally admissible policy equilibria. At $\theta=31/40$, changing only the territorial emissions-target loss curvature from $d=39/20$ to $d=41/20$ changes the subsidy-reoptimized infrastructure best-response derivative from negative to positive. The own-subsidy-held-fixed direct cross effect is positive at both points, so the sign difference is a property of joint government-policy adjustment. Exact comparisons with all nonnegative kink and monopoly deviations certify the specified symmetric equilibria, and strict margins preserve the local result in separate open neighborhoods.

This is a conditional existence result, not a characterization of the entire target-weight boundary or of all government equilibria. The complementary threshold as rivalry changes at $d=2$ and the conventional-investment benchmark do not eliminate the globality and institutional qualifications. Rival policy instruments transmit through a common competitiveness index, and the welfare benchmark permits higher emissions. The proposed economic distinction is therefore the direction of a precisely defined cross-government marginal response under different target discipline, rather than a general recommendation about green fiscal expenditure.
