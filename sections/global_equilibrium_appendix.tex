\subsection{Global-equilibrium certificate for the canonical witness}
\label{app:global-equilibrium}

The branch-specific Hessian and implicit-function calculations above do not by themselves establish a policy Nash equilibrium over the full nonnegative action space. This subsection summarizes the additional active-set argument used for the canonical witness.

Let
\[
b\equiv\frac{\mu}{k_g},\qquad
w_i\equiv m+b s_i+\nu h_i,\qquad
L\equiv2-\frac{4R}{D}.
\]
We maintain $m=a-c>0$ as stated in Section~\ref{sec:model}. After minimizing investment cost conditional on effective private cost reduction, Stage 2 reduces to a scalar choice. For $\theta>0$ the unique continuation is partitioned into five regions. Firm $A$ is a monopolist if $\theta w_A\geq(2-R)w_B$; it limit-prices with $q_B=0$ if $Lw_B\leq\theta w_A\leq(2-R)w_B$; both firms are active if $\theta w_A<Lw_B$ and $\theta w_B<Lw_A$; and the remaining two regions are the symmetric $B$-kink and $B$-monopoly cases. The formulas in Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium} coincide at each boundary.

For the rational canonical vector
\[
(k_x,k_g,\mu,\nu,\kappa,d,e,\beta,\xi,m)
=(4,18,9/10,6/5,4/5,2,11/10,17/10,1/10,2),
\]
we have $R=59/200$. Exact symbolic root counts establish for every $\theta\in[0,1]$ that the symmetric policy solution is positive, its continuation lies strictly in the duopoly region, and the duopoly own-policy Hessian is negative definite. Let $(s_B^*(\theta),h_B^*(\theta))$ be the symmetric rival policy, and write $w_B^*(\theta)=m+b s_B^*(\theta)+\nu h_B^*(\theta)$. The relevant strict exclusion inequality is
\[
F(\theta)\equiv mL(\theta)-\theta w_B^*(\theta)>0
\qquad \text{for }\theta\in[0,1].
\]
For the canonical rational parameters, $F$ is a rational function whose reduced numerator and denominator have no zeros on $[0,1]$ (after removing constant factors), and $F(1/2)>0$. This proves the inequality on the entire interval, not just at the numerical checkpoints. Because the own-policy action set is nonnegative, every own deviation satisfies $w_A=m+bs_A+\nu h_A\geq m$, and therefore $\theta w_B^*<Lm\leq Lw_A$. The rival-firm limit-pricing kink requires $\theta w_B^*\geq Lw_A$; the rival-firm monopoly requires the still stronger $\theta w_B^*\geq(2-R)w_A$, where $2-R\geq L$. Both rival-firm inactivity regions are consequently unreachable.

For the two potentially reachable outside branches, the exact own-policy Hessians are negative definite. The KKT multipliers on the common boundary have opposite signs in the required outward normals, so their constrained global maxima coincide at that boundary with strictly positive optimal subsidy and infrastructure. Exact rational-polynomial root counts establish that the symmetric duopoly objective exceeds that outside-branch maximum for all $\theta\in(0,1]$. These tests are implemented in \texttt{scripts/verify\_stage4rg\_global.py} as \texttt{lower-regimes-inaccessible}, \texttt{kink-boundary-multiplier}, \texttt{monopoly-boundary-multiplier}, and \texttt{global-policy-gap}. The zero-rivalry endpoint is treated separately because strategic exclusion is impossible there.

The own-kink and own-monopoly government problems are compared directly. Their best outside-branch value is attained at the common boundary
\[
\theta w_A=(2-R)w_B.
\]
Exact KKT signs select that common boundary, and the welfare gap between the symmetric duopoly optimum and the best exclusionary challenger is strictly positive over $\theta\in(0,1]$. Representative values are approximately $7.64866$ at $\theta=.5$, $.42775$ at $.9$, and $.07341$ at $1$. At $\theta=0$ strategic exclusion is unavailable.

For the open-neighborhood qualification, the endpoint $\theta=0$ requires an additional regularity observation. The unscaled rational representation of the exclusionary welfare gap is singular at zero, but the scaled gap $\theta^2\Delta W(\theta)$ extends continuously to $\theta=0$ and satisfies
\[
\lim_{\theta\downarrow0}\theta^2\Delta W(\theta)
=\frac{46316976555179}{8043085428750}>0.
\]
Moreover, the determinants of both the symmetric and antisymmetric blocks of the full policy first-order-condition Jacobian are root-free on $[0,1]$. Hence the equilibrium policy solution varies regularly throughout the interval. Together with strict curvature, feasibility, regime and welfare-gap margins, these facts imply by continuity a common nonempty open primitive neighborhood in which the global-SPNE configuration survives. The accompanying verification script reproduces the continuation comparisons and includes a parameter vector showing that interior curvature and switching-polynomial conditions alone need not imply global policy optimality. This example illustrates the importance of comparing deviations into exclusionary regimes.

\subsection{Limits of the no-conventional-investment comparison}

The canonical no-$x$ benchmark satisfies the positive-Bernstein condition reported above and therefore has no sign reversal. This is not a global necessity theorem. Keeping the other canonical primitives but setting $\beta=1.3$ produces a no-$x$ example with global policy equilibria in which the cross-infrastructure response is negative at $\theta=.9$ and positive at $\theta=1$, with an interior crossing near $.93949$. The repository verifies that example separately. Thus the economically valid comparison is matched-primitives separation at the canonical vector, not universal necessity of conventional investment.
