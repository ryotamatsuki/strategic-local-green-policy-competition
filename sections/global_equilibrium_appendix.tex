\subsection{Global-equilibrium certificate for the canonical witness}
\label{app:global-equilibrium}

The branch-specific Hessian and implicit-function calculations above do not by themselves establish a policy Nash equilibrium over the full nonnegative action space. This subsection summarizes the additional active-set argument used for the canonical witness.

Let
\[
b\equiv\frac{\mu}{k_g},\qquad
w_i\equiv m+b s_i+\nu h_i,\qquad
L\equiv2-\frac{4R}{D}.
\]
We maintain $m=a-c>0$ as stated in Section~\ref{sec:model}. After minimizing investment cost conditional on effective private cost reduction, Stage 2 reduces to a scalar choice. For $\theta>0$ the unique continuation is partitioned into five regions. Firm $A$ is a monopolist if $\theta w_A\geq(2-R)w_B$; it limit-prices with $q_B=0$ if $Lw_B\leq\theta w_A\leq(2-R)w_B$; both firms are active if $\theta w_A<Lw_B$ and $\theta w_B<Lw_A$; and the remaining two regions are the symmetric $B$-kink and $B$-monopoly cases. The formulas in Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium} coincide at each boundary.

For the rational canonical vector
\[
(k_x,k_g,\mu,\nu,\kappa,d,e,\beta,\xi,m)
=(4,18,9/10,6/5,4/5,2,11/10,17/10,1/10,2),
\]
we have $R=59/200$. Exact symbolic root counts establish for every $\theta\in[0,1]$ that the symmetric policy solution is positive, its continuation lies strictly in the duopoly region, and the duopoly own-policy Hessian is negative definite. Given the symmetric rival policy, nonnegative own policies cannot reach the rival-monopoly side.

The own-kink and own-monopoly government problems are compared directly. Their best outside-branch value is attained at the common boundary
\[
\theta w_A=(2-R)w_B.
\]
For the canonical rational witness, fix the rival at its symmetric policy. Use $(s_A,w_A)$ as own-policy coordinates, so that $h_A=(w_A-m-bs_A)/\nu$ and policy nonnegativity can be imposed explicitly. Conditional on the boundary equation, the one-dimensional \emph{tangential} optimization delivers strictly positive $s_A$ and $h_A$. Its tangent derivative vanishes on both branches because their value functions agree there. As $w_A$ increases through the boundary, the one-sided normal derivative is positive on the kink side and negative on the monopoly side. Together with strict concavity of each of the two outside branches, these signs put the global maximum of the exclusionary region at the common tangentially optimal boundary point, rather than at an unexamined nonlocal stationary candidate.

The final global comparison is between this boundary maximizer and the symmetric interior optimum, \emph{not} between two arbitrary first-order-condition solutions. The exact welfare gap is strictly positive throughout $\theta\in(0,1]$: representative values are approximately $7.64866$ at $\theta=.5$, $.42775$ at $.9$, and $.07341$ at $1$. At $\theta=0$ strategic exclusion is unavailable. The exact rational sign and root-count checks corresponding to the rows are implemented in \texttt{scripts/verify\_stage4rg\_global.py}; the Stage-11 independent reconstruction also checks the same policy regions by a distinct evaluator. A finite grid of numerical deviations alone would not establish these inequalities for the whole interval.

For the open-neighborhood qualification, the endpoint $\theta=0$ requires one additional regularity observation. The exclusionary boundary policies and their one-sided normal derivatives have finite limits only after multiplication by $\theta$; the corresponding unscaled welfare gap diverges as $\theta\downarrow0$. This divergence does not imply an exclusionary deviation in the independent-markets game at $\theta=0$. The scaled gap $\theta^2\Delta W(\theta)$ instead extends continuously to $\theta=0$ and satisfies
\[
\lim_{\theta\downarrow0}\theta^2\Delta W(\theta)
=\frac{46316976555179}{8043085428750}>0.
\]
Moreover, the determinants of both the symmetric and antisymmetric blocks of the full policy first-order-condition Jacobian are root-free on $[0,1]$. Hence the equilibrium policy solution varies regularly throughout the interval. Together with the strict curvature, feasibility, regime, and welfare-gap margins on the compact interval, these facts imply by continuity a common nonempty open primitive neighborhood in which the same global-SPNE configuration survives. If $\bar E=0$ is treated as the boundary of the maintained nonnegative target domain, the neighborhood assertion is relative to that admissible parameter space. The certificate establishes the existence of the specified government SPNE, not uniqueness of all government-policy equilibria. The repository script \texttt{verify\_stage4rg\_global.py} reconstructs the global comparisons independently and retains the hostile-audit parameter vector for which the old interior candidate fails as a regression test.


\subsection{Exact fixed-rivalry target-discipline certificates}
\label{app:fixed-target-global}

This subsection proves the additional two-point claim in Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed-theta-target}. It does \emph{not} extend the canonical-$d=2$ theorem to the entire $d^*(\theta)$ curve. The source-independent rational comparator and its regression tests are \texttt{docs/itpf\_final/exact\_policy\_slice.py} and \texttt{docs/itpf\_final/verify\_fixed\_theta\_result.py}. The Stage-4A/7.5A certification report fixes the exact source and computational scope; the existing Lean development certifies different, frozen statements.

Fix $\theta=31/40$ and $d\in\{39/20,41/20\}$, with all other primitives as specified in Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed-theta-target}. Holding the rival government $B$ at its symmetric candidate, let $b=\mu/k_g$, $M=2-R$, $L=2-4R/(4-\theta^2)$, $w_A=m+bs_A+\nu h_A$, and $w_B=m+bs_B+\nu h_B$. Each firm's continuation has exactly five regions, classified by the two inequalities for $w_A$:
\begin{align*}
\text{rival monopoly: }&w_A\leq\theta w_B/M,\\
\text{rival kink: }&\theta w_B/M\leq w_A\leq\theta w_B/L,\\
\text{duopoly: }&\theta w_B/L\leq w_A\leq Lw_B/\theta,\\
\text{own kink: }&Lw_B/\theta\leq w_A\leq Mw_B/\theta,\\
\text{own monopoly: }&w_A\geq Mw_B/\theta.
\end{align*}
For each branch, substitution of the unique second-stage investment continuation into $W_A$ produces a quadratic in $(s_A,h_A)$, subject to $s_A,h_A\geq0$ and the branch's affine bounds on $w_A$. Because $b,\nu>0$, every branch with a finite upper bound is bounded in policy space. The unbounded own-monopoly branch has a strictly negative-definite quadratic Hessian, making its objective coercive. A global maximum in each branch must therefore be a feasible interior stationary point, a feasible one-active-face stationary point, or an intersection of two active constraints (including the axes and kink vertices). Exact rational stationary systems, edges, and vertices are enumerated; singular face systems and empty branches are checked separately, not silently skipped.

The symmetric candidates solve the full two-government first-order conditions on the positive duopoly branch. The own Hessian is strictly negative definite and the symmetric/antisymmetric FOC blocks are nonsingular, with positive output and strictly positive policies. For the reachable own-kink and own-monopoly branches, maximizing along their common boundary $w_A=Mw_B/\theta$ gives a strictly positive policy pair. The same maximizer is a feasible boundary candidate for both branches; checking the tangential stationary condition and inward/outward one-sided normal inequalities, together with each branch's negative-definite Hessian, establishes it as the best exclusionary deviation. The duopoly candidate exceeds the best such deviation by the strictly positive rational gaps in Table~\ref{tab:two-point-global-certificates}. This proves two nonempty open neighborhoods with opposite signs, not a specified common interval of $d$. The local comparative static has no implication for arbitrary simultaneous changes in both governments' policy vectors.

The scope restriction is substantive. At $\theta=1$ and the interior sign-zero weight $d^*(1)=89308800/54845239$, the symmetric interior candidate is defeated by an own-monopoly policy deviation with welfare gain about $0.28140957$. These observations refute the identification of the whole algebraic $d^*(\theta)$ locus with a global-SPNE boundary. They do not prove that no other government equilibria exist at the offending points.


\subsection{Limits of the no-conventional-investment comparison}

The canonical no-$x$ benchmark satisfies the positive-Bernstein condition reported above and therefore has no sign reversal. This is not a global necessity theorem. Keeping the other canonical primitives but setting $\beta=1.3$ produces a no-$x$ example with global policy equilibria in which the cross-infrastructure response is negative at $\theta=.9$ and positive at $\theta=1$, with an interior crossing near $.93949$. The repository verifies that example separately. Thus the economically valid comparison is matched-primitives separation at the canonical vector, not universal necessity of conventional investment.
