## 3. Current journal evidence

### International Tax and Public Finance — PRIMARY

Current official Springer Nature scope (accessed 2026-09-05): ITPF publishes outstanding original theoretical and empirical research in all areas of public economics and explicitly identifies open-economy, international, and interjurisdictional issues as a historical strength.

Official scope: `https://link.springer.com/journal/10797/aims-and-scope`.

Recent/current fit evidence:

- Morita and Okoshi (2025), **Beneficial fiscal competition for foreign direct investment: transport infrastructure and economic integration**, ITPF 32:1839–1864: fiscal competition + infrastructure + product-market competition + threshold. This is simultaneously the strongest fit signal and the main novelty-risk comparator.
- Lai (published 2025; issue 2026), **Emission transfers, pollution havens, and environmental regulations**, ITPF: two-country imperfect-competition model with endogenous environmental policy and strategic incentives.
- Kikuchi and Tamai (2024), **Unemployment and endogenous choice on tax instruments in a tax competition model**, ITPF 31:533–551: theoretical fiscal-competition instrument choice.
- Matsumoto (2022), **Tax competition and tax base equalization in the presence of multiple tax instruments**, ITPF 29:1213–1226: multi-instrument tax competition.

Fit: **HIGH**.

Primary rationale: the paper's deepest boundary is interjurisdictional public-policy competition. ITPF readers can evaluate the difference between known cross-instrument reactions and the paper's rivalry-indexed sign-reversal theorem without requiring the environmental component to carry all novelty.

Subjective desk risk: **MEDIUM**. The main risk is perceived narrowness or ingredient overlap with Morita–Okoshi. The defense must remain theorem-level: simultaneous cross-policy best-response sign reversal indexed by product substitutability after endogenous investment, rather than infrastructure choice around MNE location and trade-cost integration.

### Journal of Environmental Economics and Management — STRETCH

Current Elsevier scope accepts theoretical and empirical environmental/resource economics, including environmental policy design/instrument choice and work intersecting industrial organization/urban economics, but requires theoretical analyses to be novel and of broad interest.

Recent fit evidence:

- Strandholm, Espinola-Arredondo, and Muñoz-García, **Greener or cheaper goods: Economies of scope in R&D investments**, JEEM 130 (2025), studies simultaneous cost-reducing and green R&D under oligopoly and regulation.

Fit: **TOPICAL HIGH; CONTRIBUTION-THRESHOLD LOW-MEDIUM**.

Subjective desk risk: **HIGH**. The theorem is clean but narrow, the global-SPNE result is an open-neighborhood certificate rather than a broad primitive characterization, and the environmental objective is territorial/delegated rather than a complete global-climate-welfare model. JEEM is therefore optional stretch only.

Elsevier's current journal AI policy requires disclosure when generative AI is used in manuscript preparation beyond basic grammar/spelling functions, with human responsibility for all content.

### Environmental and Resource Economics — REALISTIC FALLBACK

Current official Springer scope emphasizes application of economic theory and methods to environmental problems, policy responses, environmental-policy instruments, modelling, institutional arrangements, and policy relevance. It explicitly welcomes theoretical contributions, while giving high priority to empirically based policy-oriented work.

Official scope: `https://link.springer.com/journal/10640/aims-and-scope`.

Recent fit evidence:

- Gautier and Fikru (2024), **The Design of Emission Taxes in Markets with New Firm Acquisitions**: theoretical oligopoly environmental-policy design with endogenous market structure.
- Agliardi and Lambertini (2024), **To Abate, or Not to Abate? The Arising of the Win–Win Solution Under Time Consistent Emission Taxation**: endogenous abatement investment and regulation.
- Eichner and Schopf (2025), **Self-Enforcing Environmental Agreements with the Optimal Intensity of Cooperation**: clean multi-stage environmental theory using strategic-response structure.

Fit: **HIGH**.

Subjective desk risk: **LOW-MEDIUM to MEDIUM**. The environmental-policy interpretation must stay central; do not let public-finance positioning obscure the environmental question.

### Journal of Public Economic Theory — THEORY FALLBACK / SAFETY

Current Wiley scope publishes theoretical public-finance research and explicitly includes local public goods, externalities, taxation, market failure, regulation, game theory, and comparative statics; it imposes no upper/lower bound on technical complexity.

Official scope: `https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679779/homepage/productinformation.html`.

Recent fit evidence:

- Billon (2023), **Tax competition, public input, and market power**: local-jurisdiction tax competition with industrial public inputs and market power.
- Zimmermann (2024), **Public infrastructure provision in the presence of terms-of-trade effects and tax competition**: public infrastructure and tax competition.
- Kikuchi and Tamai (2024), **Nash equilibria in models of fiscal competition with unemployment**: alternative fiscal strategic variables and Nash equilibria.

Fit: **VERY HIGH on method/topic**.

Subjective desk risk: **LOW-MEDIUM**. The novelty attack is that fiscal/public-input comparative statics are known, but JPET is well suited to a narrow rigorous theorem if the exact distinction is visible.

### Regional Science and Urban Economics — EXCLUDED

Current Elsevier scope requires a meaningful spatial dimension. The frozen paper has two jurisdictions but fixed plants, no endogenous location, no distance/trade-cost geography, and no spatial equilibrium. Reframing it for RSUE would overstate the spatial content; adding location would reopen theory. Therefore RSUE is excluded.

## 5. Primary-target positioning — ITPF

The paper should be positioned as public-economics theory of **how product-market rivalry changes the direction of cross-instrument local policy responses**, with green industrial policy as the substantive application and territorial-target discipline as a scope condition.

The Introduction must state early that cross-instrument fiscal reactions are known. The most dangerous ITPF referee question is:

> Why is this not simply Hauptmeier-style multi-instrument fiscal competition plus Morita–Okoshi infrastructure competition plus Strandholm-style dual investment?

The paper's answer is fixed by Stage 11R: the contribution is the unique product-substitutability sign reversal of the same cross-instrument best response on a certified global-SPNE region, not the ingredients.
