## Strongest novelty challenge — separate the generic mathematics from the specific inference

Let `z=(s_A,h_A)`, `y=y_B`, `p=∂E_A/∂z`, and `r=∂E_A/∂y`. On the active linear-quadratic branch `H(d)=H0−d pp′` and the cross-gradient `C(d)=C0−d pr`. The profiled numerator is `N=H_sh C_s−H_ss C_h`. Its coefficient of `d²` cancels exactly since both target contributions are rank one and use the same `p`: `N(d)=N0+d N1`. Therefore a formal interior zero `d*=−N0/N1` is **generic** in this class and cannot independently establish new economic theory. HMR already covers Hessian inverse policy response and a different sign-changing reaction.

What does `not` follow from generic inverse-Hessian algebra is the value or economic admissibility of the sign at a particular set of Cournot/investment/environmental primitives. Our independent rational five-regime maximization shows that **two strict global-government SPNE profiles** at the **same** θ=31/40, changing only `d`, have opposite *infrastructure* marginal BR signs while own subsidies jointly reoptimize and both direct cross effects are positive. The positive non-duopoly gaps and policy slack make each an actual locally stable marginal comparison, not an arbitrary FOC sign. The model-specific signs and branch ordering, however, are a **bounded existence example**, not a new general welfare ranking or institutionally identified comparative-static rule.

This is the strongest available answer to the criticism *“you appended a quadratic target loss to a familiar two-policy inverse-Hessian model and solved for a zero”*: the mathematical device is old, the **strict global full-game realizability of two opposite direction examples** is newly checked, but its larger public-economic insight remains limited unless the environmental curvature can be economically or empirically distinguished. The fixed-rivalry pair makes the paper more coherent and auditable without manufacturing a generality theorem.

## ITPF relevance and remaining publisher-source risk

The [ITPF official scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/10797/aims-and-scope) explicitly covers theoretical public economics, especially open-economy, international and interjurisdictional issues. Competing local subsidies and productive public inputs with a territorial emissions objective is topical. The [official submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/10797/submission-guidelines) additionally provide a **Short Papers** collection (maximum 6,000 words excluding references and no more than five main-text exhibits, same quality standard); this is a possible **separate journal-format strategy**, not evidence of novelty or acceptance. The current full paper is not automatically a compliant short paper. Live journal-account fees and declarations are separate submission gates.
