The purpose of this repeat gate is not to ask whether the algebra is correct. It asks whether the repaired result is a sufficiently general economic mechanism to justify a new canonical theory freeze and full-paper reconstruction.

## 1. Can the core result be stated without model-specific notation?

Yes.

A compact statement is:

> When local governments have both targeted green support and a productive green public input, stronger product-market rivalry can reverse the direction of the public-input response to a rival policy shock because rivalry changes the relative strength of product-market, firm-investment, environmental-target, and within-government policy-interaction channels.

The model-specific quartic and Bernstein coefficients are proof devices, not the economic statement.

The result must not be stated as a universal claim that conventional investment is necessary for switching, nor as a subsidy-specific causal mechanism. On the interior reduced system the rival jurisdiction enters through the composite policy index

`y_B=(mu/k_g)s_B+nu h_B`,

so rival subsidy and rival infrastructure shocks share the same product-market transmission channel up to scale.

## 2. Minimal causal / strategic chain

The minimal chain is:

`rival productive green-policy shock`
` -> rival firm cost / output change`
` -> business stealing and local output change`
` -> endogenous local conventional and green investment adjustment`
` -> changes in CS, local producer surplus, territorial-target loss, and the interaction between local s and h`
` -> competition-dependent change in the sign of the local infrastructure best response`.

The key object is the switching index

`Psi(theta)=W_sAhA W_sAsB - W_sAsA W_hAsB`.

The sign of the true local infrastructure best response equals the sign of this object when the global equilibrium remains locally on the duopoly branch. Under the primitive Bernstein conditions it crosses zero exactly once.

The economic content is therefore not "high competition makes infrastructure attractive" in isolation. It is that product substitutability changes the relative weights of several strategic channels until the composite best-response index changes sign.

## 3. What assumptions are genuinely essential?

### Essential for the headline mechanism

1. `theta>0`: without product-market interaction there is no cross-jurisdictional business-stealing channel.
2. A competitiveness-relevant green-policy channel: in the benchmark `mu=0`, a rival green subsidy does not affect rival output through green investment and the corresponding cross-jurisdictional policy response vanishes.
3. Two local policy instruments that are non-equivalent in the own-government problem. Targeted subsidy and productive infrastructure must affect the government's marginal problem differently; otherwise there is no meaningful composition response.
4. An interior, regular local branch of the true global policy equilibrium around the point where the IFT comparison is made.
5. Primitive conditions ensuring that the switching polynomial has one root rather than multiple crossings.

### Important but not universally necessary

Conventional competitive investment `x_i` is an important amplification / benchmark-separation margin, but it is not necessary for every possible reversal. At the canonical primitives the full model switches while the matched no-x benchmark does not. A verified no-x counterexample at `beta=1.3` prevents any universal necessity claim.

### Normalizations / tractability

- linear differentiated demand;
- quadratic investment costs;
- symmetric canonical witness;
- one firm per jurisdiction;
- fixed plant locations;
- quadratic territorial-target loss;
- one-half allocation of integrated-market consumer surplus across equal-sized jurisdictions.

These assumptions deliver closed form and a tractable switching index. The Stage-7 robustness evidence shows that the sign-reversal mechanism is not tied exclusively to Cournot competition or complete local profit capture.

## 4. Does the contribution survive an alternative formulation?

Yes, with an explicitly local scope.

Under differentiated Bertrand competition, using the same primitives, the interior cross-infrastructure response is negative at `theta=.3`, positive at `theta=.5`, and crosses near `theta_B*=.3972`. The relevant equilibria are positive and locally strictly concave around the crossing.

The Bertrand infrastructure choice reaches the nonnegativity boundary around `theta=.545`, so the robustness result is not a global theorem over the full rivalry interval. That limitation is acceptable for the Stage-7.5 question: it establishes that quantity competition is not the unique source of the sign reversal.

With partial local capture `omega=.9`, the interior Cournot response also changes sign near `theta*=.5111`. This is evidence against complete-local-ownership dependence, not a theorem for arbitrary ownership leakage.

## 5. Is there a clear welfare / organizational implication?

Yes, but it is secondary and must remain narrow.

The decentralized-versus-coordinated first-order-condition wedge is

`Omega_zA = dW_B/dz_A`

`= 1/2 CS_zA + PS_B,zA - d(E_B-Ebar_B)E_B,zA`.

Hence decentralization omits real cross-jurisdictional consumer-surplus, producer-surplus / business-stealing, and territorial-target effects. This is not subsidy-transfer accounting.

At the canonical `theta=.9` example, coordination raises model welfare and changes fiscal composition toward infrastructure, but it also raises output and territorial emissions. Therefore the valid interpretation is second-best regional-welfare coordination, not a general theorem that coordination lowers emissions or corrects a universal infrastructure under-provision problem.

The old dimensionally invalid statistic `h/(s+h)` is permanently excluded. Descriptive fiscal composition may use the common-unit outlay share

`phi_h=(kappa h^2/2)/(s g+kappa h^2/2)`.

## 6. Would a skeptical field referee understand why this is not merely a parameter exercise?

Yes, provided the manuscript is rebuilt around the repaired theorem rather than the old architecture claim.

The result is more than a numerical threshold because:

- the cross-response is reduced analytically to a primitive polynomial in `theta^2`;
- Bernstein coefficient restrictions provide primitive sufficient conditions for a unique crossing;
- Stage 4R-G separately proves that the canonical candidate is a true global policy equilibrium and that the result survives on a nonempty open set of primitives;
- the hostile counterexample is retained and demonstrates why interior FOCs alone are insufficient;
- the matched no-x benchmark supplies a same-primitives separation without being overinterpreted as universal necessity;
- the sign reversal survives a differentiated-Bertrand formulation on an interior open set.

The remaining novelty risk is real: Hauptmeier-type multi-instrument reaction functions, Morita-Okoshi subsidy/infrastructure thresholds, and Strandholm-type dual investment are all prior art. The paper must therefore sell the theorem-level product-substitutability sign reversal and its global-SPNE validation, not the ingredients.

## 7. Does rival-policy proportionality undermine the contribution?

No, but it narrows the framing.

Because the rival policy vector enters the interior continuation through `y_B`, the model does not identify a unique rival-subsidy transmission mechanism. A rival infrastructure shock produces the same sign pattern up to the scale factor `nu k_g/mu` on the same branch.

This means the valid contribution is a competition-induced **policy-composition response** to a rival productive green-policy shock, with the rival subsidy used as the headline cross-instrument normalization. It is not a theorem that subsidies are strategically special relative to rival infrastructure.

The own-government policy pair remains economically non-equivalent because `s_A` and `h_A` enter firm investment, infrastructure resource cost, emissions, and government welfare differently. Hence the switching of the own response vector remains substantive rather than a relabeling exercise.

## 8. Is the matched no-x benchmark sufficient after the necessity claim is withdrawn?

Yes, as supporting mechanism evidence rather than as the sole contribution.

The headline theorem does not require a universal no-x impossibility result. The matched benchmark answers a narrower causal question: at the same canonical environmental and policy primitives, does adding the conventional-investment adjustment margin change the qualitative best-response network? The answer is yes.

The verified `beta=1.3` no-x reversal is therefore not a fatal objection. It fixes the quantifier: conventional investment can generate a reversal absent in the corresponding benchmark; it is not necessary for all reversals.

The paper must avoid titles, abstracts, proposition labels, and discussion language that imply universal necessity.

## 9. Freeze constraints implied by this gate

A new Stage-8 freeze must incorporate all of the following:

1. Explicit nonnegative action sets `s_i,h_i,x_i,g_i,q_i >= 0`.
2. Full Stage-2/3 continuation across duopoly, limit-pricing kink, and monopoly regions.
3. The canonical global-SPNE/open-neighborhood certificate as part of the equilibrium claim.
4. Headline Proposition: unique product-substitutability threshold for the local infrastructure response on the true global-equilibrium branch under stated primitive conditions.
5. Matched no-x benchmark separation only; no universal dual-investment necessity language.
6. `mu=0` as a competitiveness-link benchmark, not a novelty claim.
7. Welfare interpretation as territorial-target / second-best regional welfare, not global carbon damages.
8. Fiscal outlay share in common units only; never `h/(s+h)`.
9. Bertrand and partial-ownership results described as local open-set robustness, not global theorems.
10. Empirical prediction framed as nonlinear / threshold policy reaction, not a globally positive linear interaction.
11. Rival-policy proportionality disclosed explicitly; no subsidy-specific structural interpretation unsupported by the reduced system.
12. Closest-paper boundary from Stage 6 retained: classification `DISTINCT BUT NARROW`.

A title containing "through dual investment" should be reconsidered at Stage 8 because the no-x counterexample makes that phrase easy to read as a necessity claim. A safer working title is:

`Strategic Local Green Policy Competition: Product-Market Rivalry and Instrument Switching`.

This is a positioning change, not a theory change.

## 10. Stage-7.5 verdict

`GO`.

The repaired project has a generalizable strategic mechanism rather than only a technically correct parameter example. The global-equilibrium objection has been closed on a nonempty open region, the theorem survives result-level novelty re-kill, the welfare object is coherent after correction, and the sign reversal survives at least one alternative product-market formulation.

The contribution remains narrow and must be presented accordingly. The paper should not be sold as the first model of multi-instrument green federalism, as a universal dual-investment necessity result, or as a theorem that stronger competition monotonically increases infrastructure responses.
