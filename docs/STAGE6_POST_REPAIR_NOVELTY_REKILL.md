## 1. Repaired proposition set submitted to the novelty gate

The post-repair contribution candidate is deliberately narrower than the pre-audit version.

### Proposition candidate A — competition-induced cross-instrument sign reversal

On a nonempty open primitive region containing the canonical witness, the true global-SPNE policy equilibrium is interior and remains on the duopoly continuation locally.  Along that equilibrium branch, the best-response derivative

`d h_A^BR / d s_B`

changes sign exactly once as product substitutability `theta` rises: it is negative below a unique interior threshold and positive above it.  At the canonical witness the threshold is approximately

`theta* = 0.7738043861`.

The general local sign theorem is proved by the quartic/Bernstein sufficient conditions; Stage 4R-G separately supplies the global-SPNE/open-set bridge.

### Proposition candidate B — matched nested-benchmark separation

For the same canonical primitives, removing conventional competitive investment `x_i` eliminates the reversal over `theta in (0,1]`.  Thus adding the conventional-investment margin can create a switching result that is absent in the corresponding matched benchmark.

This is **not** a necessity theorem.  Stage 4R-G verifies a no-`x` counterexample with `beta=1.3` in which global policy equilibria exhibit negative cross response at `theta=.9` and positive cross response at `theta=1`.  Hence conventional investment is not necessary for every possible reversal.

### Proposition candidate C — competitiveness-link benchmark

At `mu=0`, a rival green subsidy no longer transmits through product-market competitiveness and the cross-jurisdictional policy response vanishes in the benchmark.  This remains mechanism validation, not a standalone novelty claim.

## 2. Result-level hostile search

Searches were targeted at the repaired objects:

- tax/subsidy versus productive public-input cross responses;
- parameter-dependent sign changes in cross-instrument reactions;
- subsidy/infrastructure competition under Cournot competition;

The search did not use the absence of identical keywords as evidence of novelty.  Each material paper was compared on players, timing, strategic variables, continuation game, and theorem object.

## 3. Closest-paper reconstruction

### Hauptmeier, Mittermaier, and Rincke (2012), Regional Science and Urban Economics 42(3), 407–419

This remains the closest paper for the **reaction-function architecture**.

Their theoretical model has two symmetric jurisdictions simultaneously choosing a business tax and a productive public input to compete for perfectly mobile capital.  The public input raises capital productivity.  They explicitly derive a two-dimensional implicit-function system for government reaction functions and show cross-instrument responses: a rival tax cut induces both an own tax cut and higher own public input, while rival infrastructure also affects both own instruments.

Therefore the following are not contributions of SLGPC:

- simultaneous two-instrument jurisdictional competition;
- using a productive public input as an alternative competitiveness instrument;
- the 2x2 IFT formula for policy reactions;
- cross-instrument response per se;
- parameter-dependent signs of multi-instrument reactions in general.

However, Hauptmeier et al. have no oligopolistic product-market rivalry parameter, no firm-level conventional and green investment stage, no green-investment subsidy, and no theorem in which product substitutability uniquely reverses the sign of the infrastructure response to a rival green subsidy.  Their cross-response expressions are driven by mobile-capital allocation and public-input productivity.  The repaired SLGPC threshold is therefore not an immediate corollary of their theorem.

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`, not absorbing.

### Morita and Okoshi (2025), International Tax and Public Finance 32, 1839–1864

This remains the closest modern paper for the **subsidy + infrastructure + Cournot + threshold** combination.

Their game is sequential: governments first choose transport infrastructure, then fiscal offers, then an MNE chooses location, and finally firms compete à la Cournot.  Their main threshold asks whether fiscal competition raises or lowers infrastructure as infrastructure-independent trade costs/economic integration change.  The fiscal subsidy is tied to discrete MNE location and the public input lowers trade costs.

This kills any broad claim that subsidy competition plus infrastructure under Cournot, or a threshold determining whether fiscal competition expands infrastructure, is new.

It does not derive the simultaneous-policy cross-best-response derivative `d h_A^BR/ds_B`; product differentiation is not the threshold variable; firms do not choose conventional and green investment portfolios; and the paper's threshold is a comparison between fiscal-competition and no-fiscal-competition infrastructure levels rather than a sign reversal in a rival-policy derivative.

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`, not absorbing.

### Strandholm, Espinola-Arredondo, and Muñoz-García (2025), JEEM 130, 103107

Their firms simultaneously choose cost-reducing R&D and green/abatement R&D, followed by an environmental regulator and Cournot competition.  They establish strategic interactions between the two investment margins and show that treating only one R&D margin can misstate optimal regulation.

This kills novelty for dual conventional/green investment, for the idea that the two investment margins interact through oligopoly, and for general claims that product-market concentration changes environmental regulation through R&D incentives.

They have a single regulator, no competing local-government policy portfolios, no productive public infrastructure instrument, and no cross-jurisdictional policy reaction theorem.

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

### Bayindir-Upmann (1995/1998) and related environmental-federalism work

The 1995 working paper integrates interjurisdictional tax competition, emission taxation, and two local public goods, including an industrial public good.  It already shows that decentralized competition can generate over- or under-provision of public goods and inefficient environmental quality.  The 1998 European Journal of Political Economy paper studies interjurisdictional emission-tax competition when local firms compete imperfectly.

These papers kill broad novelty claims about multi-instrument environmental federalism, policy-composition distortion, environmental policy competition under imperfect competition, and public-input/environmental-policy links.

They do not absorb the repaired product-substitutability threshold or matched firm-investment benchmark separation.

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP / STRUCTURALLY RELATED`.

### Markusen, Morey, and Olewiler (1993, 1995)

These papers are now especially relevant after Stage 4R-G because they emphasize that environmental policy can change endogenous market structure, plant operation, and location, producing discontinuities that invalidate purely marginal interior analysis.  The 1995 paper explicitly allows regional policy competition to drive a polluting firm from a market.

They therefore provide important prior art for the **boundary-regime warning** exposed by the hostile audit.  Stage 4R-G's duopoly/kink/monopoly continuation is a correctness repair, not a novelty contribution.

They do not study the repaired simultaneous subsidy/infrastructure policy portfolio with firms choosing conventional and green investment, nor the unique product-substitutability cross-response threshold.

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`, highly relevant for equilibrium discipline.

### Keen and Marchand (1997), Hindriks, Peralta, and Weber (2008), and related fiscal-competition work

Keen and Marchand establish systematic distortions in the composition of public expenditure under capital-tax competition.  Hindriks et al. study regions competing in capital taxes and productivity-enhancing public investment.  These papers further eliminate any broad claim that fiscal competition distorts productive public investment or policy composition.

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP`.

### Recent 2026 climate/industrial-policy models

The search also considered recent work on fragmented climate regulation, green subsidies, product differentiation, and industrial-policy competition.  These papers reinforce that strategic environmental policy, R&D, product differentiation, and fragmented regulators are active literatures.  No paper located in this pass combines the repaired SLGPC timing and strategic variables or states an equivalent product-substitutability cross-response theorem.  They are not used as positive novelty evidence; their role is to prevent an artificially narrow search universe.

Classification: `MERELY RELATED / COMPONENT OVERLAP` depending on paper.

## 4. Whole-game absorption test

Repaired SLGPC architecture:

`(s_A,h_A),(s_B,h_B)`
` -> (x_A,g_A),(x_B,g_B)`
` -> nonnegative differentiated-Cournot continuation`

with governments simultaneously choosing a firm-specific green-investment subsidy and productive green infrastructure, firms choosing conventional cost reduction plus green investment, and the continuation admitting duopoly/kink/monopoly regimes.

No reviewed paper contains this whole architecture **and** an equivalent theorem that product substitutability uniquely changes the sign of the infrastructure response to a rival green subsidy.

Whole-game absorption verdict: `NOT FOUND`.

This is not sufficient by itself for novelty; the theorem-level test below is controlling.

## 5. Theorem-level absorption test

### Broad theorem: cross-instrument response can differ from same-instrument matching

`KILLED` by Hauptmeier et al. and related fiscal-competition theory.

### Broad theorem: subsidy competition can increase or decrease infrastructure depending on a parameter threshold

`KILLED` by Morita and Okoshi (2025).

### Broad theorem: conventional and green R&D jointly alter environmental-policy incentives

`KILLED` by Strandholm et al. (2025) and earlier strategic environmental-policy/R&D work.

### Narrow theorem: product-market substitutability induces a unique negative-to-positive sign reversal in `d h_A^BR/ds_B` in a local green-policy game with endogenous conventional and green investment

`SURVIVES` this re-kill.  No equivalent theorem or immediate-corollary mapping was found.

The distinction from Hauptmeier et al. is not that their model lacks a second policy instrument; it does not.  The distinction is the transmission system from rival green subsidy through firm investment portfolios and oligopolistic business stealing, with `theta` entering the continuation technology and crossing a unique sign threshold.

The distinction from Morita–Okoshi is not that their model lacks both subsidy and infrastructure; it has both.  Their timing and theorem object differ: their threshold governs whether the presence of fiscal competition raises infrastructure under different trade-cost conditions, not the sign of a simultaneous cross-policy best response as product substitutability changes.

### Matched benchmark result: adding conventional investment creates a reversal absent under the same canonical primitives without that margin

`SURVIVES`, but only as a **matched benchmark separation**, not as a universal necessity theorem.

The no-`x`, `beta=1.3` global-equilibrium counterexample permanently bars language such as "conventional investment is necessary for switching" or "dual investment is the minimal architecture for every reversal."

## 6. Contribution statement after re-kill

Permissible core statement:

> Existing fiscal-competition models already show that governments respond across tax/subsidy and public-input instruments, and environmental-IO models already show that firms jointly choose conventional and green investment.  In the repaired model, combining these margins with differentiated product-market competition yields a sharper result: over a nonempty global-SPNE region, increasing product substitutability uniquely reverses the sign of a local government's infrastructure response to a rival green-investment subsidy.  For the matched canonical primitives, this reversal is absent when the conventional-investment margin is removed.

Do not claim:

- first model of local green-policy competition;
- first multi-instrument environmental-federalism model;
- first cross-instrument reaction function;
- first subsidy/infrastructure interaction;
- first threshold linking fiscal competition and infrastructure;
- first conventional-plus-green R&D model;
- universal necessity of conventional investment;
- novelty of the duopoly/kink/monopoly repair itself.

## 7. Novelty risk assessment

The project remains `DISTINCT BUT NARROW`.

Secondary risk: Morita–Okoshi makes any broad "subsidy competition changes infrastructure at a threshold" framing untenable.

The Stage-4R-G global-equilibrium repair improves correctness but does not increase novelty.
