## Acquisition-before-scoring rule and inspection protocol

Only legitimately accessible **complete public journal HTML** together with its material proofs/appendices, or an equivalent complete lawful copy, may be selected. Article title/abstract, search results, inaccessible journal links, partial previews and lecture slides are NOT full text. Inspection covered the article from Introduction through Conclusion, its proposition/method/result paragraphs, figures and relevant proof appendices. The source URLs below are access locators; the copyrighted prose is not reproduced. Full-text acquisition/read claims are made only for the three selected articles, and no scores are assigned to excluded or partially inspected papers. Reading notes identify observable structural moves, not copied sentences.

## Observed source locations and reusable techniques (original adaptation only)

1. **Morita–Okoshi**, Introduction §1 opening and research questions: begin with familiar policy intuition, pose an apparent counterexample, and ask a precise policy-choice question *before* the apparatus. §3 Fiscal Policy Competition: distinguish strategic fiscal-price incentives from infrastructure spillovers; §4 Discussions/figures and §5 Conclusion: interpretation following analytic conditions, location and transport scope explicit. Online Appendix A–G: keep proof-heavy and extensions separate, with claims in the main body and no apparent license to import unproved globality.
2. **Matsumoto**, Introduction §1: contrast existing single-instrument result with what adding an instrument does to that implication, without claiming the instrument itself is novel. Central result/lemma and associated derivative steps in the body, **Appendix A.1–A.8** for expansions: visible proof-critical bridge, routine algebra moved off-page. Conclusion: concise distinction between efficiency and political redistributive objectives.
3. **Lai**, Introduction §1: begin with a contested directional prediction and identify opposing mechanisms before introducing endogenous policy. Main comparative-static propositions and figures: cases shown under stated domains, not interpolated as universal theorems. Discussion §8 and Conclusion: make emissions-accounting differences and caveats decision-relevant. Integrated Appendices 1–6: detailed mathematical audit trail retained without hiding headline economics.
