# Literature Boundary Ledger

This file records the contribution boundary established by the Stage 2 and Stage 6 novelty gates. Bibliographic metadata must be verified before use in the manuscript.

## Closest literature families

### Multi-instrument interjurisdictional competition

Hauptmeier, Mittermaier, and Rincke (2012), *Fiscal competition over taxes and public inputs*.

Known overlap: governments simultaneously use multiple instruments and exhibit same- and cross-instrument strategic responses. Their theoretical model explicitly derives a two-dimensional reaction-function system for taxes and productive public inputs. Therefore simultaneous two-instrument competition, the 2x2 IFT reaction formula, cross-instrument response, and flexible instrument substitution are not contributions.

### Environmental federalism / policy composition

Bayındır-Upmann (1990s environmental-federalism/public-input work), Keen and Marchand (1997), and related fiscal-federalism work.

Known overlap: decentralized competition can distort environmental/fiscal policy and the composition of productive public spending. Therefore policy-composition distortion, over/under-provision of public inputs, and environmental-policy competition under imperfect competition are not contributions.

### Subsidy and infrastructure competition

Morita and Okoshi (2025), *Beneficial fiscal competition for foreign direct investment: transport infrastructure and economic integration*.

Known overlap: fiscal subsidy competition and endogenous public infrastructure interact with subsequent Cournot competition, and a parameter threshold determines whether fiscal competition raises or lowers infrastructure. Therefore subsidy-infrastructure interaction, Cournot transmission, or a threshold governing the infrastructure effect of fiscal competition is not a contribution.

### Conventional and green investment

Strandholm, Espinola-Arredondo, and Muñoz-García (2025), *Greener or cheaper goods: Economies of scope in R&D investments*, JEEM 130, 103107.

Known overlap: firms jointly choose conventional cost-reducing and green/abatement investment under oligopoly/environmental regulation. Therefore dual investment itself, strategic interaction between those investment margins, and general claims that dual R&D alters optimal environmental regulation are not contributions.

### Boundary / market-structure discipline

Markusen, Morey, and Olewiler (1993, 1995).

Known overlap: environmental policy can change plant operation, location, and market structure, generating discontinuities and making purely marginal interior policy analysis incomplete. The Stage-4R-G duopoly/kink/monopoly repair is therefore a correctness requirement, not a novelty contribution.

## Surviving claim after Stage 4R-G and the repeated Stage 6 gate

The surviving contribution is deliberately narrow:

1. On a nonempty open region of true global-SPNE equilibria, product-market substitutability can induce a unique negative-to-positive sign reversal in a local government's green-infrastructure best response to a rival's firm-specific green-investment subsidy.
2. For the matched canonical primitives, that reversal is absent when conventional competitive investment is removed.

The second statement is a matched benchmark separation only. It is **not** a universal necessity claim: with no conventional investment and `beta=1.3`, the repaired model has global policy equilibria with a negative cross response at `theta=.9` and a positive cross response at `theta=1`.

Do not claim that conventional investment is necessary for every possible switching result, that dual investment is a novel component, or that the full architecture is novel merely because no paper has identical primitives.

Post-repair Stage 6 classification: `DISTINCT BUT NARROW`; theorem-level absorption not found.
