# Literature Boundary Ledger

This file records the contribution boundary established by the Stage 2 and Stage 6 novelty gates. Bibliographic metadata must be verified before use in the manuscript.

## Closest literature families

### Multi-instrument interjurisdictional competition

Hauptmeier, Mittermaier, and Rincke (2012), *Fiscal competition over taxes and public inputs*.

Known overlap: governments use multiple instruments and exhibit cross-instrument strategic responses. Therefore cross-instrument response itself is not a contribution.

### Environmental federalism / policy composition

Bayındır-Upmann (1990s environmental-federalism/public-input work).

Known overlap: decentralized competition can distort the composition of fiscal/environmental policy. Therefore policy-composition distortion itself is not a contribution.

### Subsidy and infrastructure competition

Morita and Okoshi (2025), subsidy/infrastructure competition with Cournot and threshold effects.

Known overlap: subsidy and infrastructure instruments can interact strategically under product-market competition. Therefore subsidy-infrastructure interaction or a threshold per se is not a contribution.

### Conventional and green investment

Strandholm, Espinola-Arredondo, and Muñoz-García (2025), conventional cost-reducing and green R&D under oligopoly/environmental regulation.

Known overlap: firms jointly choose conventional and green investment. Therefore dual investment itself is not a contribution.

## Surviving claim

The surviving contribution is the joint-architecture result: product-market rivalry can reverse the sign of a government's green-infrastructure response to a rival's firm-specific green subsidy, and the reversal disappears in the nested benchmark without conventional competitive investment.
