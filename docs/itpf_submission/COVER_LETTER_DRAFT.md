Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript “Green Subsidies, Public Infrastructure, and Product-Market Rivalry: When Cross-Instrument Responses Reverse” for publication as a regular research article in *International Tax and Public Finance*.

The paper studies how a local government adjusts productive infrastructure when a competing jurisdiction marginally increases a firm-facing green-investment subsidy. The government can also reoptimize its own subsidy, while firms make conventional and green investments before differentiated Cournot competition. The resulting infrastructure response is a conditional cross-policy best-response derivative, not the effect of a simultaneous change in all government policies.

The central result holds product-market substitutability fixed at 31/40 and changes only the curvature of a territorial emissions-target loss. At target-loss weights 39/20 and 41/20, the subsidy-reoptimized infrastructure responses have opposite signs even though the direct cross effect is positive in both economies. Exact-rational checks against nonnegative kink, monopoly and boundary policy deviations establish a strict global best response at each of the two specified symmetric equilibria, with separate local neighborhoods of validity.

The paper distinguishes this controlled result from the established literature on multi-instrument fiscal competition and from models comparing infrastructure levels across fiscal regimes. It therefore offers a narrowly quantified theoretical implication for interjurisdictional public finance with environmental objectives, while explicitly identifying the limits of globality, the common rival-competitiveness transmission index, and the welfare-versus-emissions distinction.
