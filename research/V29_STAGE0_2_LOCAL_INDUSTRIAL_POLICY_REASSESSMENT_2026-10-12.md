## Stage 0 — phenomenon, decisions, competing mechanisms

Phenomenon: Higher governments fund industrial initiatives nominated by local governments under scarce national budgets. Proposal quality, expected local benefits, public infrastructure needs and interactions with other regions differ. Potentially the submitted menu itself depends on expected acceptance.

Decisions: Central authority chooses project solicitation, grant/portfolio and budget; regional governments choose what, if anything, to prepare and propose; contractor and private firm choices matter only if necessary to answer a specific question; public budget constraint and welfare/incidence are explicit. The private market option must be a real no-policy benchmark, not presumed inefficient.

Potential mechanisms and distinct fingerprints:
- M1: Externality/location decision rights across governmental levels — old parent.
- M2: Private local project values/costs and grants — old parent.
- M3: Strategic competition for scarce project funding and cheap talk — old parent.
- M4: Complementarity/substitutability among projects — old parent.
- M5: Locally costly non-contractible PREPARATION of project candidates — old parent in single-project governance; possible interaction with portfolio.
- M6: Selection of which candidates are available for national comparison (endogenous nomination/discovery) — potential narrow residual, not yet novel.
- M7: Empirically induced extra infrastructure from subsidy contest — mixed/empirical, separate theory pipeline.

Stage 0 one-sentence hypothesis: Under some explicitly testable institutional conditions, can the endogenous selection of which public-industrial projects are discovered and nominated change the optimal assignment of final project-decision authority compared with a model in which the list of candidates is fixed? This is a QUESTION, not theorem.
