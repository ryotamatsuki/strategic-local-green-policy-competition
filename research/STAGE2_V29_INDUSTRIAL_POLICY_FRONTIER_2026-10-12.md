## Application-neutral parent reduction attempt

The broad hierarchy question compares central internalization of spillovers, local informational advantage and distortions from strategic competition. In a simple quadratic benevolent-planner environment, optimal allocation of intervention across levels follows a standard covariance/spillover/incentive-cost trade-off. P03 already proves explicit **necessary-and-sufficient** conditions for central dominance in a one-project, private-information, rights-and-bargaining case, and P01/P02 address industrial policy more directly. Therefore 'add an information parameter and show a threshold for centralization' is a generic reparameterization, NOT novelty. A real candidate must prove a theorem changing the **allocation rule or feasibility of a contract** due to something not nested in these parents, rather than only applying the old condition.

For input-output networks, P13 and P14 already propagate subsidies upstream/downstream in spatial/general equilibrium; network terms in a tax/subsidy welfare FOC alone are also not new. Any Q1 model must first show that interaction with *decentralized information acquisition and choice of generalized vs targeted government instrument* yields a nonseparable, economically important new implementability/ranking theorem not reducible to P01/P02/P03/P04 or P14. No such theorem has been proved yet.

## Priority opportunity hypotheses — question-first, not invented models

### Q1 — theoretical, CONDITIONAL FRONTIER LEAD
**Question:** When firms have private information on technological fit/costs that regional governments may learn locally, and public industrial inputs create production-network spillovers across regions, when (if ever) is a central targeted-firm subsidy inferior to decentralized investment in general-purpose productive infrastructure or to a split-assignment regime, even after allowing the center a suitable information mechanism?

**What is newly learned if solved:** a nontrivial condition on information rent/credibility **and incidence of upstream versus downstream spillovers** determining who should provide **which instrument**, not a generic 'spillovers centralize; information decentralizes' criterion. Prove a counterexample or theorem in which the assignment actually changes an instrument choice, implementation feasibility, or a non-obvious welfare ranking relative to existing parent result.

**Killer references:** P01, P02, P03, P04, P07 and P14, plus current mechanism-design parents (risk extremely high). Even a plausible solution may be a direct corollary of a rich parent model.

**Smallest required test BEFORE architecture:** obtain/read Theorem/Appendix in Caillaud 1996 and Morasch 2003, and explicitly express P03 Theorem 5.1/5.2 and Hevia 2023 policies under the proposed minimal information/externality structure. Seek the **one missing strategic choice** with a plausible non-absorbed economic conclusion, not a list of extra variables. If no such choice, KILL Q1. **Status `LITERATURE-SCREENED / UNRESOLVED`**, no model GO.

### Q2 — empirical/mixed-method alternative, PARTIALLY SCREENED
**Question:** In real interlocal firm-attraction contests where the final firm location is unchanged, does a firm subsidy induce *incremental* productive transport infrastructure and positive **net** welfare for host and nonhost after accounting for counterfactual projects, budget burdens, commuting, displacement and supply-chain connectivity?

**New learning if solved:** distinguish pure transfer/budget competition from the **induced-infrastructure channel** in Morita–Okoshi Proposition 1–2; provide heterogeneous treatment effects on infrastructure and broad welfare rather than narrow employment-only benefits.

**Killer references:** P08's theory, P12/P13's causal subsidy and supply-chain spillover empirics, P19/OECD firm microeconometrics. Cannot claim novelty of spillover estimation generally. First check actual Japanese (or suitably comparable) grant/firm/location contenders and independent infrastructure timing; if firm bids and project-specific capital outlays unobservable, NO-GO for causal identification. **Status `HYPOTHESIS / UNRESOLVED`**. Methodological home is an empirical research workflow, not automatic theory Stage 3.

### Q3 — explicit author future question, DEFERRED / NOT YET CREDIBLE
Klibanoff–Poitevin (2022) explicitly recommend alternative bargaining protocols at §6/p.438. However Morasch (2003) and classic mechanism-design work already cover regional bargaining, side payments, information exchange and delegation. 'Consider symmetric Nash bargaining instead of take-it-or-leave-it' risks triviality and lack of implementability. Unless a practical contracting constraint causes a new **general** welfare or governance ranking after reading these parents, keep `DEFERRED/UNRESOLVED`, **NOT** a third fabricated positive candidate.
