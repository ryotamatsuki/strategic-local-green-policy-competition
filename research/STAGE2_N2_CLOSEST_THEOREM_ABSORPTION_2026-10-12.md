## Original-source primary theorem comparison

### P01 Caillaud–Jullien–Picard (1996); SAME-AUTHOR 1994 full preprint
Bibliography: 'National vs European incentive policies: Bargaining, information and coordination', *European Economic Review* 40:91–111, DOI `10.1016/0014-2921(95)00045-3`. Publisher final HTML: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0014292195000453
**Full 1994 original/ancestor working-paper version**: *National vs European industrial policies: A contract theory approach*, CEPREMAP N°9401, 39 scanned pages, https://www.cepremap.fr/depot/couv_orange/co9401.pdf . Do not falsely call its theorem numbering the exact revised journal pagination; journal PDF not fully accessible. 1994 abstract and results substantively match the publisher final abstract, but theorem identity not certified line by line.

**Actually proved in the 1994 full paper (Theorems 1–5):**
- National-local regulator has more precise information/monitoring about unobservable private producer activity; agents act with moral hazard, impact multiple regions; central agency sets producer-directed incentives/compensatory interregional transfers; national regulator and firm Nash-bargain the national supplement.
- **Theorem 1** (simple moral hazard) characterizes central/national contribution to incentives and suboptimal firm action after nested bargaining.
- **Theorem 2** gives an explicit sufficient condition for full decentralization under marginal information-rent/cost criteria; national authorities supply incentives while the center can set compensatory interregional transfers.
- **Theorem 3** shows **full centralization is never optimal under THEIR simple moral hazard and strong local-information assumptions**; NOT a universal theorem for all jurisdictions/monitoring environments.
- **Theorem 4** re-establishes the partial decentralized action result for nested informational asymmetry.
- **Theorem 5** allows general communication/revelation mechanisms but finds the same optimal implementable allocation as restricted non-revelation policies under the analyzed arrangement.
- The center and subnational incentive programs already coexist and can complement one another; the paper already frames applicability to national-versus-region fiscal federalism, not only EU institutions (1994 Introduction p.6).

**Author-stated explicit limitations/future research: 1994 preprint §6 'Concluding Remarks', printed p.36 / PDF page index 36**:
1. what changes if *contingent compensating transfers among member states are impossible*;
2. endogenize authority / negotiate a Pareto-efficient, individually rational charter specifying agency powers;
3. account for **horizontal strategic interactions**, e.g. public policy commitment and secret renegotiation.
These are author's, not auditor's, examples. They are old openings and have numerous later policy bargaining/location parents (Morasch 2003/2008, Markusen 1995, Klibanoff–Poitevin 2022); **none is certified still open in 2026**. Parent differentiation remains a mandatory test.

**Absorption:** local information + private firms' hidden action + externalities + BOTH central/local complementary firm incentive regimes are already WHOLE-GAME partially integrated. Rebranding a subsidy 'green' and introducing production networks cannot alone clear novelty. **Exact targeted subsidies vs general infrastructure in an IO network are not jointly present in this inspected 1994 game**, so call `BROADER CORE SOLVED; exact Q1 result UNFORMULATED`, not 'identical theorem'.

### P02 Morasch (2003); later closely matched Bartholomae–Morasch (2008) full paper
Original 2003: *Deciding about (de-)centralization of industrial policy: Delegation by a central authority vs. bargaining of regional governments*, Bundeswehr University Munich WP 2003/3, https://ideas.repec.org/p/zbw/ubwwpe/20033.html . Its EconStor PDF access was **blocked by automated browser protection**; therefore exact 2003 internal proposition numbering is not verified.
**Accessible authorship-continuation full text:** Florian Bartholomae & Karl Morasch (2008), *Assigning the Power to Perform Strategic Trade Policy in an Economic Union*, 24-page full paper https://www.etsg.org/ETSG2008/Papers/Bartholomae-Morasch.pdf, especially Abstract, §§2–4, Table 2 and §5 Conclusion. Do not identify this separate 2008 paper as a verbatim copy of the 2003 WP.

**Established economics from accessible 2008 text**:
- Integrated Cournot duopoly, cross-border rents and strategic industrial policy.
- Central decision authority has weaker industry-specific firm-cost signal than regional governments.
- Original allocation of authority: central unilateral delegation vs local governments bargaining to centralize. Information may be disclosed *in the bargaining stage* and used in subsequent policies.
- Under no new information exchange, delegation can outperform bargaining; offering **side payments** may cause delays/disagreement through strategic bargaining.
- With signals revealing information, regional bargaining can outperform central delegation in expected welfare within the studied parameterization. A simple 'government with more info wins' theorem is false as a general economic slogan.

**Author-stated bounded open comparison: Bartholomae–Morasch 2008 §5 Conclusion p.22/PDF p.22**: the combination of *bargaining-stage information transfer AND side payments* was not tractable in the model; the headline information-transfer result excludes side payments. This is not automatically new: 1994 Caillaud already uses interregional compensations under information, Klibanoff–Poitevin model rights/participation/transfers, and general bargaining mechanism theory could cover it.

**Absorption:** game-theoretic hierarchy choice, incomplete info, oligopoly business stealing, government bargaining, side-payment/information revelation interaction are already analyzed. The 2003 abstract plus 2008 full text make Q1's central headline highly derivative, even though a precise network-specific instrument assignment is not literally solved.

### P03 Klibanoff & Poitevin (2022), JPET 24:417–451
*A theory of (de)centralization*, DOI `10.1111/jpet.12572`, full author-hosted journal PDF https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/klibanof/ftp/workpap/klibanoff-poitevin_decentralization.pdf .
**Exact journal Theorems 5.1–5.2, pp.433–434, visually inspected:**
- Two locally informed entities with project externality, centralized authority without enforceable vertical commitments versus distributed property rights and take-it-or-leave-it regional bargaining/transfers.
- Under theorem-specific conditions, centralized governance is better than ALL compared decentralized rights regimes iff the local-benefit to externality **variance ratio** satisfies `1/3 < sigma_theta² / sigma_gamma² < 3`; under the second regime additional **expected externality lower bound** is needed.
- When central governance is better, the chosen central project policy is **uniform** under their endogenous cheap-talk/screening restriction. Theorems also assign bargaining-power/decision-right configurations by private-information dispersion.
- These are **conditional** necessary/sufficient claims, not universal verdicts across all economic structures.

**Author-stated limitation in §6 Conclusion, journal p.438 (PDF index 21)**: local bargaining assumed take-it-or-leave-it; broader bargaining protocols, equal shared bargaining power and credible implementing procedures remain for further study. **Not automatically open** given Morasch and general mechanism-design parents.

**Absorption:** general information-versus-externality centralization/rights and participation restriction are rigorous established results, and simply adding a covariance or a different network parameter does not constitute independent novelty.

### P04 Hevia, Loayza & Meza-Cuadra (2023), Economía 46:39–52
*Industrial Policies vs Public Goods under Asymmetric Information*, DOI `10.18800/economia.202301.003`. Full publisher PDF https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/economia/article/download/27317/25588/108441 .
**Verified original model/result (§3, §4, printed pp.43–50):**
- Central planner initially endowed with `T`, chooses firm-specific subsidies `s1,s2` and a broad public good `g`, budget `g+s1+s2<=T`; two heterogeneous productivity firms privately observe productivity.
- Linear firms' output value `v_j=z_j(k0+s_j+alpha*g)`; α under 1. Truth-eliciting targeted **firm-specific tax + subsidy** mechanism can attain first best despite private information when sufficiently strong administrative capabilities exist.
- With low-capacity simple policies and sufficiently effective public input (`alpha>1/2` for their two-firm equal subsidy comparator), public input dominates subsidy-to-claims/flat subsidies. This is conditional on the paper's model, not universal public-goods always dominant.
- Hence the policy choice *targeted vs general public input under private information and differing administrative capacities* is DIRECTLY solved already; not enough to say that combining this with government hierarchy is new.

**AUTHOR-STATED §4 Conclusion, printed p.50/PDF index 11**: (i) more general production functions incl complementarity between public input and capital, (ii) **costly state verification or imperfect monitoring** are explicit future research possibilities. Such future work may be solved by older principal-agent and public-works contract literatures, e.g. *Local public works and intergovernmental transfers under asymmetric information* JPubE 2004 and externality-information regional provision works; recheck before promoting.

**Absorption:** one-government targeted firm support vs broad productive public input with private firm types and fiscal budget is already a complete analyzed result. Its administrative-capacity/budget distinction matters if proposing a different hierarchical split of *monitoring vs financing*, but neither a theorem nor a distinct welfare prediction for that split has been formulated in Q1.

Publisher abstract only, no precise theorem-number proof read.

Institutional local/central assignment under private local information not claimed solved, but Q1's 'network targeting' element is NOT distinctive.
- **OECD (2025)** and **IMF (2025/2026)** industrial policy reports integrate firm heterogeneity, cost/benefit and upstream/downstream spillovers, so the intended policy relevance of the network interaction is also not wholly unexplored.
