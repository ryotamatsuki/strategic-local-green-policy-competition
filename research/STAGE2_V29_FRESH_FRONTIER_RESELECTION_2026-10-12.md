## 0. What changed in the evidence since previous source register

Most important **new** postpublication finding: **OECD 2025 'Place-based industrial policy: Lessons for place transformation'** openly said that the **national perspective on territorializing industrial strategies** was outside its report and was assigned to a **forthcoming companion study**. That companion **was published on 2026-10-09**: Eduardo Rodriguez Montemayor, **'Targeting places in national industrial strategies,' OECD Regional Development Paper no.184**, DOI `10.1787/284fcc80-en`. It provides a place-diagnostic toolkit, place-sector mapping and national/local co-design roadmap. Therefore it would be false to call 'how to map national industrial goals to local capabilities' an *unanswered OECD agenda item* on 2026-10-12. These papers are diagnostic/roadmap, not a proof of full optimal dynamic contract theory, but a plain place-diagnostic proposal is **KILLED**. Another strong parent: Pablo Fajgelbaum & Cecile Gaubert, **'Place-Based Policies: Lessons from Theory'**, NBER w33517 (Feb/Aug 2025; 2026 volume indicated), https://doi.org/10.3386/w33517. **Abstract lists SIX theory results** including national wage subsidies under uniform positive agglomeration elasticity, region-optimal labor subsidy equal to spillover elasticity, when low-wage areas can be targeted, and inability of spending on infrastructure, investment incentives, or housing to fully correct labor-density externalities. Therefore 'which place gets subsidy and when does infrastructure correct an agglomeration externality?' is **already highly solved**. Full theorem proof not read in this pass: do not overextend these results beyond model.

A further strong empirical prior: **Stephen Roper, Enrico Vanino & Nola Hewitt-Dundas (2025)**, *Industrial and Corporate Change*, DOI `10.1093/icc/dtae046`, estimates dynamic complementary outcomes of national UKRI, Northern Ireland regional, and UK Catapult sectoral R&D support. Therefore 'national/local innovation grants may be complements' is **already documented** (source full abstract; methods matching/event-study, not necessarily causal interactions fully identified). OECD 2026 addresses institutionally coordinating such programs from the place-sector diagnostic perspective.

## 3. ECONOMIC-QUESTION OPPORTUNITY SHORTLIST (no claimed novelty certification)

### F1 — PRIORITY FOR ONE SHORT THEOREM-ABSORPTION TEST (THEORY/HYBRID)
**Question:** For a geographically targeted industrial subsidy, when may continuation and clawback rules based on the **auditable host-region firm employment/investment outcome** systematically select the WRONG projects from the perspective of **national** surplus because the measured local gains partly displace production or fiscal resources from OTHER regions? What minimal additional **verifiable cross-region performance measure**, if any, permits a budget-limited public authority to implement (or closely approximate) the socially preferred continuation decision, considering strategic recipient/host responses?

This is *not* a general claim that national social spillovers, performance targets, optimal auditing, or contracting under asymmetric information are new. The **putative new learning** would be a useful explicit **observability/implementability boundary** for choosing *which aggregate outcome must be contracted on*, and a condition under which local-job conditionality misallocates scarce subsidy euros **even if all firms perfectly comply**. Potential testable *question target*, not a proved proposition: is there a nontrivial smallest aggregation across recipient, same-sector neighbors and upstream/downstream firms necessary/sufficient to rank projects consistently, and does this change the public audit-versus-payments mix? Need more than a trivial 'include the externality' statement.

**Sources combined, and direct kill threats**: AUTHOR Mazzucato–Rodrik 2026 distinguishes causal additionality vs overall public value and does not estimate either in case taxonomy; AUTHOR Rodríguez-Pose 2026 stresses adaptive monitoring, but it is normative. Slattery 2025 (subsidy auction welfare), Cingano 2025 (selection welfare under rules), Siegloch 2025 (subsidy cross-county effects), Morita–Okoshi 2025 (host/nonhost infrastructure welfare), Fajgelbaum–Gaubert 2025 (spatial optimal policy), and **Holmström / multi-task principal-agent informativeness & mechanism-design** are **STRONG absorption threats**. In a simple separable welfare model the requirement to count neighboring externality is a textbook corollary. The candidate must yield a different **contract implementability**, endogenous metric manipulation or monitoring-value theorem, with transparent primitive restrictions.

**Status**: `HYPOTHESIS / UNRESOLVED / NOT AUTHORIZED`; **highest priority for a one-session N3 LITERATURE-ONLY KILL**, not for immediate game coding, Stage-3 approval or full-paper planning. It may fail N3.

### F2 — EMPIRICAL RESEARCH QUESTION, AN ALTERNATE TRACK
**Question:** How much of the host and neighboring jurisdictions' **net** benefit from a competitive firm-attraction subsidy arises **causally because the contest triggered an otherwise unplanned productive transport/public-input investment**, as opposed to relabeling planned capital works or simply relocating firms and profits? How does that benefit compare against the fiscal financing wedge and displacement?

**Potential new learning**: direct counterfactual identification of Morita–Okoshi's 2025 infrastructure-mediated welfare mechanism, rather than another treated-firm job estimate. To warrant general importance distinguish projects that are genuine induced additionality, national fiscal transfers, displaced activity and nonhost welfare. **Empirical design must demonstrate a legal/program rule, bid contest or discontinuity with project-level public works and applicant/counterfactual data**; mere spatial DiD of subsidy receipts cannot identify induced infrastructure without strong assumptions.

**Kill papers**: Slattery 2025 (bidding), Siegloch–Wehrhöfer–Etzel 2025 AEJ:EP (nearby/trade connected effects), v. Ehrlich & Seidel 2018 (public-investment persistence + relocation), Cingano et al. 2025 Econometrica (grant cost per new job), OECD 2025 firm subsidies, plus recent 2026 energy community place-targeting welfare work (Doshi et al. SSRN; abstract only).

**Status**: `AUTHOR-EXPLICIT 2025 MORITA OUTLOOK / CROSS-PAPER / HYPOTHESIS, UNRESOLVED`; potentially strong scientific/policy contribution but **not feasible until project/grant/bid/public-works data are validated**. It would require a causal evaluation workflow, not the pure-theory Stage-3 mechanism sequence.
